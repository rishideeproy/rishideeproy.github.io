About

I am a Lecturer in the School of Computing and Mathematical Sciences at the University of Leicester. My research lies at the intersection of probability theory and statistical modelling, with applications to real-world forecasting problems.

I work in two broad areas: the theoretical study of stochastic processes — including log-correlated Gaussian fields, interacting particle systems, and branching random walks — and applied statistical modelling, where I develop Bayesian methods for forecasting and decision-making, with particular focus on sports analytics and categorical time series.

Before joining Leicester, I was a Lecturer at the University of Essex and an Assistant Professor of Decision Sciences at IIM Bangalore. I completed my PhD in Statistics at the University of Chicago, where I worked on extreme values of log-correlated Gaussian fields, and my undergraduate and master’s studies at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata.
